## Measured architectural rationale

Sprint 1's supplied decision observed native German `Feler` and English `sentense` spelling feedback at first observations around 0.4 seconds, with replacement candidates through macOS. It reported nlprule 0.6.4 at about 0.72 ms p50 / 0.91 ms p95 and 372 MiB for 1/8 additional non-spelling detections, and LanguageTool 6.8 at about 4.38 ms p50 / 20.47 ms p95 and 646 MiB for 7/8. These are supplied study results, not measurements repeated in Sprint 2 or universal benchmarks.

Native spelling adds zero runtime; explicit Gemma supplies deeper judgment. Additional grammar engines therefore remain deferred for the initial daily-use successor. The published predecessor ledger separately records the qualified model's roughly 8.43 GB resident estimate and dynamic cold-admission limitation on the 16 GB Mac. Warm qualification is not an availability promise.

## Completion and future milestones

The current milestone ends only when the exact installed V0.2 release candidate satisfies resource, native spelling, safe editing and real-Mac acceptance and its identities/evidence are published. Sprint 2's documentation completion alone makes no daily-use readiness claim.

Observed daily use may later justify one automatic grammar fast lane; broader contenteditable/rich surfaces or multiple suggestions; additional explicit rewriting actions; a deterministic CLI followed by MCP; system-wide macOS integration; a keyboard shortcut or floating button. Each requires its own versioned objective and observed value. LanguageTool/nlprule remain candidates, not current dependencies. No native scaffold, speculative interface, oMLX fork/resident-only operation, commercial feature or generalized support claim belongs to this freeze.
